Live-action macro coffee commercial. Single continuous 6-second take, no cuts.

Motion: Two thin glossy dark-amber espresso streams pour continuously from the chrome portafilter spouts down into the ceramic cup. The streams are already flowing when the shot begins and are still flowing when it ends — they never start, stop, sputter or break. Where the streams strike the surface, the hazelnut crema churns and marbles in slow swirling turbulence, fine bubbles forming and drifting outward. The liquid level in the cup rises slowly and only slightly over the six seconds. The crema surface ripples gently and settles.

Steam: Thin delicate wisps of steam rise continuously from the crema surface and from the spouts, curling slowly upward through the warm backlight and dissolving into the dark background. The steam stays clearly visible throughout the shot.

Camera: One smooth continuous slow dolly track from left to right with a very slight push-in toward the cup. Locked-off, mechanical smoothness, no handheld shake, no orbit, no rack focus. Shallow macro depth of field is held on the crema surface and the spout tips; the background stays soft warm bokeh.

Lighting stays constant: warm 3000K amber key, hard rim backlight separating the steam and putting a specular edge on the chrome and the ceramic lip, deep brown-to-black background falloff. No lighting changes, no flicker.

Everything else holds exactly as in the first frame: same cup, same portafilter, same tabletop, same framing, same warm grade.
