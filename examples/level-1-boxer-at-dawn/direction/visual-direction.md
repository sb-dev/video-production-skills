# Visual Direction — Boxer at Dawn

## Output
6s, 16:9, single shot, gritty-but-polished sports cinematography. No dialogue, no audio track.

## Subject
One boxer, mid-training, striking a hanging heavy bag.

- Build: lean, muscular, visibly conditioned — an athlete, not a bodybuilder
- Wardrobe: black training shorts, bare torso, hand wraps under worn black training gloves
- State: sweat-slicked shoulders, back and forearms; chalk dust on the wraps and hands
- Stance: orthodox, weight forward on the ball of the back foot, guard high between punches
- Face: focused, jaw set, breathing hard — effort, not aggression-posturing

Anatomy is the hardest constraint: two hands, five fingers, correct limb count, correct
joint articulation through the punch. Frame to reduce anatomical risk — chest-up-to-mid-thigh
coverage, hands legible, no extreme foreshortening.

## Environment
- Simple boxing gym: bare concrete floor, one heavy bag on a chain from an unseen ceiling mount
- Background: dark, deep, mostly falling to black — one suggestion of a far window with pale dawn light
- No clutter: no other bags, no ropes, no signage, no text, no spectators, no equipment piles

## Lighting
- Low-key, high-contrast, single dominant hard-ish key from camera left/behind — cold dawn
  daylight raking through a high window
- Key rims the boxer's shoulder, deltoid, forearm and the near face of the bag
- Sweat and chalk read as specular hits and airborne dust motes in the light beam
- Deep shadow fills the rest of the frame; blacks crushed but not clipped
- Practical spill (a single dim overhead gym lamp) allowed as a weak warm counterpoint
- No flicker, no strobing, no lens flares

## Camera
- Slow handheld push-in — restrained forward move, ~10–15% of frame width over the 6s
- Micro-shake only: breathing-level, documentary weight. No whip, no jitter, no roll
- Height: chest level, slight low angle to give the boxer mass
- 35–50mm feel, medium shot, shallow-ish depth of field, focus held on the boxer
- Framing: boxer left-of-centre or right-of-centre, bag occupying the opposing third

## Motion
- 2–3 punches inside 6s — readable, not a flurry. Each strike lands with visible consequence
- Body rotation drives the punch: rear foot pivots, hip turns, shoulder follows, arm extends last
- Bag reacts: compresses on impact, swings back on its chain, momentum carries between strikes
- Sweat and chalk dust displaced by impact
- Between punches the guard returns; the boxer resets. No floating, no dance

## Colour / Tone
- Desaturated, cool-neutral base (concrete grey, cold blue dawn) with warm skin as the only
  saturated element
- High contrast, deep blacks, controlled highlight roll-off on sweat
- Fine film grain — organic, not clean-digital
- Mood reference: modern boxing documentary photography and sports commercial work
  (Deakins-style naturalism, not stylised hyper-grade)

## Negative direction (avoid)
- No text, logos, watermarks, subtitles, or signage anywhere
- No second person, no coach, no crowd
- No slow-motion look — this is real-time physical motion
- No warping or morphing of hands, gloves, or the bag through the punch
- No chaotic camera, no orbit, no crane, no rack focus
- No clutter, no bright fill, no flat lighting
- No unnatural sweat sheen or plastic skin

## Success criteria
1. The punch reads as a punch: rotation → extension → contact → bag reaction, in that order
2. Anatomy holds through the fastest frame of motion (hands, gloves, elbows, knees)
3. Bag has weight — it deforms and swings on a plausible arc, not a rubbery wobble
4. One clear directional key light with sweat/chalk catching it
5. Camera reads as handheld operator, not as generative drift or warp
6. Composition stays clean: subject, bag, darkness, nothing else
