# Artifact Readiness

Apply only the criteria relevant to the artifact's role.

- **Storyboard:** sequence understandable, shot purpose clear, placement coherent, no material continuity contradiction, inherits approved character/wardrobe/environment references, ready for shot planning. A board contradicting its own parents is not ready, however well drawn.
- **Reference frame:** composition resolved, approved references preserved, identity/product fidelity sufficient, usable as a shot target. Every subject that must move is posed mid-action rather than diagrammed; a subject standing still here will stand still in the shot.
- **Motion prototype:** action, camera and timing testable; no gross motion defect invalidates the concept.
- **Video shot:** intended action and purpose achieved, technically usable, **motion plausible** — no periodic seams, no frozen frames, plausible gait and ground contact — continuity sufficient, editorially usable. A shot that stages the story perfectly and slides its feet is not usable.
- **Edit:** sequence complete, message/story understandable against supplied intent, pacing coherent, no obvious missing coverage, no unintended letterboxing from unconformed sources.
- **Master:** approved edit represented, required audio/finishing present, technical QC passed, motion pass passed.

## Approval

A creative artifact is not ready on the strength of the producing agent's own judgement.

Check that `approved` and `locked` states record an approver. An artifact an agent advanced to `approved` on its own authority is not approved; it is selected, and downstream production should not rely on it.

## Drafts

A draft is ready when it can support its current decision; it does not need final polish.
