# Reference Frames

A reference frame is the approved visual target for one shot.

Build it from the most specific available approved inputs:

```text
visual direction
+ storyboard frame
+ shot plan
+ character/product/environment references
```

Use alternatives only when comparison is useful. Once selected, refine from the selected parent and preserve accepted identity, product, environment, composition, framing, lighting and styling.

Prefer reference-frame conditioning when it reduces the amount of composition and continuity the video model must invent at the same time as motion.

## A photograph of a moment, not a blocking diagram

The frame becomes the video model's first frame, so it is read as physical state, not as intent.

- **Pose every subject that must move mid-motion.** Weight transfer, heel and toe, arm swing. A subject standing still in the reference will stand still in the shot, and no prompt wording will overcome it. If a shot depends on two subjects moving in opposite directions, both must be mid-stride in the frame.
- **Avoid diagram compositions.** Subjects in matched lateral profile, symmetrically placed, at equal scale, with a clear void of ground between them is a staging chart rendered photoreally. It does not read as photography, and the emptiness is not something motion generation will fill.
- **Occupy the space the way the location would be occupied.** An empty floor in the middle of a rush hour reads as a set.
- **Verify against the shot plan's action, not only its composition.** Composition review asks "is this the right frame"; action review asks "does this frame contain the state the motion has to continue from".

## The environment is its own artifact

Design and select the environment reference before shot frames derive from it.

The location is the most reused asset in a multi-shot production. Promoting whichever candidate happened to be generated first to "the environment" propagates its defects — garbled signage, an unreadable clock, a landmark in the wrong place — into every downstream frame, and those defects are then expensive to reopen.

## Frames carry known generation defects

Check the frame for artifacts that will persist into motion, and record any you accept:

- pseudo-text on signage, boards and displays;
- illegible clock or instrument faces;
- landmark relationships that contradict another approved frame.

Illegible text is not a safe substitute for readable text. Shifting glyphs are a visible artifact, and describing them as a continuity safeguard is a rationalisation. If text must not be legible, keep it out of frame or defocus it deliberately.
